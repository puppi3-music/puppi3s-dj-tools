Mixes and clips

For your own recorded mixes: analyze <mix> --tracklist checks a recording against what you played in Traktor, and adds YouTube chapters, clip ideas and pieces long enough to post on their own. mixes adds up all your analysed mixes. clips cuts the clips from the finished video, and clips describe <analysis> <clips> describes clips someone else cut, for the posts.

Before you start

  • Run every command from the project folder, as uv run dj-track-analyzer <command>. See Running a command.
  • Any file over 12 minutes is treated as a DJ mix by analyze: it gets a timeline and a readable summary after the JSON. The options on this page add the rest.
  • The tracklist check needs Traktor's history file of the session. Traktor writes it when it closes, so close Traktor once after the set. Its own recordings carry their start time in the name (Recording_2026-01-31_22h00m00s.wav); for any other name give --start.
  • Scan your music first (scan): the per-track check compares the mix with each track alone. With a tempo view near the tempo you played, it also shows what your mixing changed.
  • clips and clips describe <analysis> <clips> need ffmpeg. The Setup tab installs it; or brew install ffmpeg on a Mac, winget install ffmpeg on Windows, sudo apt install ffmpeg on Linux.
  • Everything here is fine with your DJ app open: it only reads Traktor's history and collection.

On the web page, the Mixes tab's Analyse a mix… does all of this with ticks, and saves each result in logs/, and All N mixes is mixes. The guide's My recorded mixes walks through it.

analyze <mix> --tracklist

Checks a recorded mix against Traktor's history of that session: each track placed in the recording, with its key, tempo and mood as played.

  • Fine with your DJ app open; reads Traktor's history only
  • Changes: nothing (with --out: that file)
  • Needs: a recording, Traktor's history file, a normal scan
uv run dj-track-analyzer analyze "/path/to/Recording_2026-01-31_22h00m00s.wav" --tracklist
uv run dj-track-analyzer analyze "/path/to/mix.wav" --tracklist --start 22:00:00 --out logs/mix.json
  • Key: Traktor's key against the key heard in the middle of the track's main stretch. Long blends are normal, so it measures how long after Traktor logs a start a track takes over, and judges from there. A mismatch can still be the other deck; a track with under 30 s of its own gets no verdict. Each change of track is rated key to key: same key, one step, two steps or a clash.
  • Mood: the track's mood as played, next to the track alone and, with a tempo view, the track at that tempo.
  • Key lock: history files don't record it. It's taken as on, unless the keys fit key lock off clearly better.
  • Genre: how the mix sounds as played, with the speed-up and your mixing (stems, layers, effects) counted. Only the models' habit of calling almost any sped-up track psytrance is held back, as in the tempo view.
  • When no history file covers the recording, the mix is analysed without the check and the result says why, so --tracklist works on any file. A history file you name, or --chapters, has to work, or it stops.
  • The JSON is printed, then the readable summary; --json-only leaves the summary out. Save it with --out into logs/ to see it on the Mixes tab and in mixes.

Options

OptionWhat it doesDefault
--tracklist [HISTORY.nml]Checks the mix against Traktor's history. Without a path it finds the file from the recording's date.off; found
--start HH:MM:SSWhen the recording started, for a file whose name doesn't say.from the name

Chapters and clip ideas

For posting the mix as a video: YouTube chapter lines for the description, and the best stretches for short clips.

  • Fine with your DJ app open
  • Changes: nothing (with --out: that file)
  • Needs: the offset, read off the finished video
uv run dj-track-analyzer analyze "/path/to/recording.wav" --tracklist --chapters --offset 0:14
uv run dj-track-analyzer analyze "/path/to/recording.wav" --tracklist --offset 0:14 --highlights 5 --out logs/mix.json
  • The offset is how far into the finished video the first track starts. Check it in the video itself: it depends on how you lined the audio up, so it can't come from the recording. Trimming silence off the audio doesn't change the times; they count from the first track's start.
  • Chapters: the video's opening becomes an "Intro" chapter at 0:00, and very short plays fold into the next chapter (YouTube wants at least three, 10 s apart).
  • Clip ideas are loud stretches rising out of something quieter, ranked #1 best, in video times with --offset. That's loudness only, so pick by eye and snap to a bar; clips adds how much moves on screen.

Options

OptionWhat it doesDefault
--chaptersPrints YouTube chapter lines. Needs --tracklist and --offset.off
--offset SECONDSWhere the first track starts in the finished video (14 or 0:14), for chapter and clip times.none
--highlights [N]Suggests the N best stretches for short clips.off; 2
--clip-length SECONDSLength of each suggested clip.40
--clip-mood LUAlso favours clips that sound party or aggressive, worth up to this many LU of loudness. Try 3.0 (loudness and rise only)

Standalone pieces

With --tracklist, the analysis also looks for long blends that work as a track of their own, to post on SoundCloud.

  • Fine with your DJ app open
  • Changes: nothing; on by default with --tracklist
  • Needs: the original tracks on disk; stem files help
uv run dj-track-analyzer analyze "/path/to/recording.wav" --tracklist --standalone-separate
uv run dj-track-analyzer analyze "/path/to/recording.wav" --tracklist --no-standalone
  • A piece is a change where two or more tracks (the two of the change, and the track before or after them) play near their own level for 2:30 or more. Blends of 64 bars or more that fall short of that are listed as short edits, a moment to post on its own.
  • Near means no more than 12 dB under the track's own level (6 dB with --standalone-style clean), or lower (down to 16 dB under) but steady, as a track played bass-down or filtered is.
  • A track with a stem file is measured per stem, so a live stem cut or bass swap lowers it by that stem's share instead of reading as a fade. Without one it's measured whole, unless you add --standalone-separate.
  • It also looks for solo reworks: one track alone, played changed (EQ, stems or re-arranged) in half its bars or more.
  • No piece is a valid answer: then the result shows the closest change.

Options

OptionWhat it doesDefault
--no-standaloneDoesn't look for standalone pieces.off
--standalone-separateSplits tracks without a stem file into stems with Demucs over the stretch where they play. Slow, a few minutes a track; needs the stems extra.off
--standalone-pairs-onlyMeasures only the two tracks of each change, not the track before and after. A run of three or more tracks then isn't seen.off
--no-solo-reworkDoesn't look for solo reworks.off
--standalone-style blend|cleanHow you mix. blend: you blend most of the time, so a track counts as playing up to 12 dB under its own level. clean: you mostly make clean changes, so only up to 6 dB counts and only blends with both tracks near full level are found. A solo rework needs the other tracks further down than that.blend

mixes

Adds up all your analysed mixes: the shape of your sets, keys Traktor may have wrong, mood as played, mood by fifth of the set and how your key changes go.

  • Fine with your DJ app open
  • Changes: nothing
  • Needs: mix analyses saved in logs/ (by the page or --out)
uv run dj-track-analyzer mixes
uv run dj-track-analyzer mixes --logs ~/old-mixes --logs logs
  • Only the newest analysis of each recording counts.
  • Your sets start to end: each set stretched to the same length, the mean hype and loudness per stretch, and the colour you played there most.
  • Keys Traktor may have wrong: tracks whose key in your sets is our key but not Traktor's, with key lock on. Worth checking in Traktor.
  • Mood as played for each track, against the track alone and in the tempo view; mood by fifth of the set.
  • Changes of track: how often the key change from one track to the next was the same key, one step, two steps or a clash.
  • Mixes analysed before November 2026 count in fully once analysed again.

Options

OptionWhat it doesDefault
--logs FOLDERThe folder with the saved mix analyses. Repeat it for several.logs/
--jsonPrints the report as JSON.off

clips

Cuts quick previews of every clip suggestion from the finished video so you can watch and choose, then the clips you pick at full quality.

  • Fine with your DJ app open
  • Changes: preview files; with --pick, clip files next to the video
  • Needs: ffmpeg, and an analysis made with --highlights and --offset
uv run dj-track-analyzer clips logs/mix.json --video final.mp4
uv run dj-track-analyzer clips logs/mix.json --video final.mp4 --pick 1 4 20:30-21:10
  • Previews are 720p, in a folder named after the analysis. While cutting, it measures how much moves on screen in each, and ranks them by the audio and the movement together. Files start with that rank (#1 clip-1 7m37s.mp4). The audio alone can't see a stretch where the DJ stands still.
  • --pick takes suggestion numbers or your own ranges. Clips are 1440p30 H.264 (the hardware encoder on a Mac, software elsewhere), named <video>-clip-1.mp4, or -clip-20m30s-21m10s.mp4 for a range.
  • Clips already there are never replaced without --force.

Options

OptionWhat it doesDefault
--video FILEThe finished video the offset was read off. Required.none
--pick N …Makes these clips at full quality: suggestion numbers (1 4) or own ranges (7:35-8:15).previews of all
--previews DIRFolder for the previews; they go in a subfolder named after the analysis./tmp/clip-previews
--out DIRWhere the --pick clips go.next to the video
--height PXThe --pick clips' height in pixels.1440
--bitrate RATEThe --pick clips' video bitrate.16M
--forceReplaces --pick clips that are already there.off
--dry-runLists what would be made, writes nothing.off

clips describe <analysis> <clips>

When an editor cuts the clips, gives them back to the analyzer for the posts: where each sits in the mix, the track playing, and how it sounds.

  • Fine with your DJ app open
  • Changes: nothing; with --save, the analysis' JSON
  • Needs: ffmpeg, the mix's analysis and its recording
uv run dj-track-analyzer clips describe logs/mix.json "/path/to/clips"
uv run dj-track-analyzer clips describe logs/mix.json clip-1.mp4 clip-2.mp4 --offset 0:14 --at 2=41:20 --save
  • Each clip is found in the recording by its sound, so it works however the editor trimmed the audio. About 15 seconds a clip.
  • Each gets a line like the clip suggestions: its video time, the track playing, LUFS, rise and energy, plus its own genre, top moods and BPM from the clip's own sound. Use an analysis made with --tracklist for the track names.
  • Video times need the offset: --offset, or it's worked out from the analysis' clip suggestions when they have video times.
  • A clip whose sound isn't clearly in the recording says so (another mix, or a phone's room sound). Place it by hand with --at: clip 2 starts 41:20 into the video. Without an offset the time is a mix time.

Options

OptionWhat it doesDefault
--recording FILEThe recording the analysis was made from, if it has moved.the file named in the JSON
--offset SECONDSWhere the first track starts in the finished video, for video times.from the clip suggestions
--at CLIP=TIMEPlaces a clip by hand where its sound isn't found, e.g. 2=41:20. Repeat for several.found by sound
--saveAlso keeps the result in the analysis' JSON, shown on the page.off
--jsonPrints JSON instead of the readable lines.off
