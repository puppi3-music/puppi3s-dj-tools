Genres and tags

The commands that put genres into your files and your DJ app, learn your own genres, and keep or set a track's values by hand: tag, untag, regenre, train, traktor, hold and edit.

Before you start

  • Run every command from the project folder, as uv run dj-track-analyzer <command>. See Running a command.
  • All of them work on scanned tracks: run scan first. Nothing here analyses audio again, except train for example tracks it hasn't heard yet.
  • The usual order: scan, check the genres_multi column in data/collection.csv, then tag for the files and traktor for your DJ app.
  • Everything that writes has --dry-run (except hold and train, which only change the app's own files) and an undo: untag for the files, traktor --undo for your DJ app.
  • Quit your DJ app before traktor and edit: they refuse while Traktor runs.

On the web page, the same things live on the Setup tab (Genre tags in your files, Genres, colours and Smartlists in Traktor, Learn your genres) and in each track's Edit. The guide's Genres for Traktor and Navidrome walks through it.

tag

Writes the scanned genres, and if you like the BPM and key, into your music files' own tags, for Navidrome, Lexicon and other players. No new analysis.

  • Changes: your music files' tags
  • Undo: untag
  • Needs: a scan
uv run dj-track-analyzer tag --multi --dry-run
uv run dj-track-analyzer tag --multi
  • Before a file's tags change for the first time, the old genre, BPM and key are saved in the app's database, so untag can put them back.
  • Tracks whose genre is held keep the genre they have.
  • Give folders to tag only the tracks in them: tag "/path/to/New tracks".
  • Players pick the new tags up on their next library scan.

Options

--fields FIELDSWhat to write, comma-separated: any of genre, bpm, key, camelot (key or camelot, not both). Example: genre,bpm,camelot.genre
--multiAlso adds close runner-up genres, e.g. Acid Trance; Hard Trance.off
--multi-ratio RWith --multi: a runner-up needs at least this share of the top genre's score.0.7
--max-genres NWith --multi: the most genres per track.3

--use-my-genreWrites your trained genres instead, where one fits. The genre switch on Both or Mine does this always.off
--dry-runShows what would change without touching any file.off

untag

Puts back the genre, BPM and key tags your files had before this app first changed them.

  • Changes: your music files' tags
  • Needs: an earlier tag, or a scan with --write-tags
uv run dj-track-analyzer untag --dry-run
uv run dj-track-analyzer untag
  • Only files the app changed are touched. Give folders to restore only the files in them.
  • Your DJ app keeps what traktor wrote; undo that with traktor --undo.

Options

OptionWhat it doesDefault
FOLDEROnly the files in these folders.every changed file
--dry-runShows what would change without touching any file.off

regenre

Works out every scanned track's genres again from the saved analysis and the current genre rules, in seconds, without decoding any audio.

  • Fine with your DJ app open
  • Changes: the app's files only (database and spreadsheet)
  • Needs: a scan
uv run dj-track-analyzer regenre
  • Run it after editing data/dj_genres.json, or after an update changed the built-in genre list. styles checks your edits first.
  • It gives the same genres a new scan would. Genres you set with edit are kept.
  • Files and your DJ app only change when you run tag and traktor afterwards.

No options.

train

Learns your own genres from example tracks, for genres the built-in ones don't have or your own way of splitting them. Also sets the genre switch: which genres your tracks get.

  • Fine with your DJ app open (but quit it so new playlists are saved)
  • Changes: the app's files only
  • Needs: a scan; 20–50 example tracks per genre, at least two genres
uv run dj-track-analyzer train "/path/to/Genre examples"
uv run dj-track-analyzer train --use both

Give the examples one way:

  • A folder with one subfolder, or one .m3u8 playlist, per genre. Each is a genre named after it.
  • A folder with --by-tag: each file's own genre tag is its genre; subfolders are searched too.
  • --traktor-playlist once per playlist, or --traktor-genre once per value of Traktor's Genre field.
  • It also learns what "everything else" sounds like from the rest of your scanned collection, so it needs at least 30 other tracks.
  • Only example tracks it hasn't heard before are analysed, so training again after adding examples is quick.
  • It prints how it did per genre on your own tracks. Both and Mine only need 30 example tracks with at least 10 in each genre.
  • The page's version: Your own genres in the guide.

Options

OptionWhat it doesDefault
FOLDERThe folder of examples: one subfolder or .m3u8 playlist per genre.none
--by-tagThe folder's files are grouped by the genre in their own tags instead of by subfolder.off
--traktor-playlist PLAYLISTLearns from this Traktor playlist, a genre named after it. Repeat for each, at least 2. In folders: "DJ / Hyperpop".none
--traktor-genre GENRELearns from the tracks with this value in Traktor's Genre field. Repeat for each, at least 2.none
--use MODEThe genre switch. starter: the built-in genres only. both: yours where one fits, built-in elsewhere. mine: the nearest of yours on every track. Works alone or with examples.unchanged; starter at first

Give one source of examples (a folder, --traktor-playlist or --traktor-genre), or only --use.

traktor

Puts the genres into your DJ app's collection, gives uncoloured tracks their predicted colour, and builds a Genres (Track Analyzer) Smartlist folder with one Smartlist per genre. Works on the DJ app you chose (Traktor or Rekordbox, where they are smart playlists).

  • Quit your DJ app
  • Changes: your DJ app's collection (backup first)
  • Undo: traktor --undo
  • Needs: a scan, and tag unless --genres-from-analysis
uv run dj-track-analyzer traktor --dry-run
uv run dj-track-analyzer traktor
  • The genres come from the files' tags, as tag wrote them. With --genres-from-analysis they come straight from the analysis instead and the files are left alone.
  • Colours you set yourself stay, unless --overwrite-colors. Held genres and colours stay as they are.
  • Smartlists are made only for the app's genres (and your trained ones), not for every tag a shop put in.
  • Edits only the parts it changes, never the whole file. Tested with Traktor Pro 4.5.
  • --undo refuses if the collection changed since this app wrote it, so Traktor's own later changes aren't lost. --force restores anyway.

Options

--dry-runShows what would change without touching anything.off
--genres-from-analysisGenres straight from the analysis, as tag --multi picks them, leaving the files' tags alone.off
--smartlists-onlyOnly (re)creates the Smartlists; genres stay.off

hold

Keeps tracks' genre or colour as they are, so tag, traktor, the tempo view and the Lexicon and Rekordbox writes leave them alone.

  • Fine with your DJ app open
  • Changes: the app's hold list only
  • Undo: --release
uv run dj-track-analyzer hold --genre "part of the name"
uv run dj-track-analyzer hold
  • Picks the scanned tracks whose path contains the text, in any case. At most 10 at a time, so a short typo can't hold half the collection.
  • hold alone lists what's held, with the value and since when.
  • A genre or colour set with edit is held already.
  • The page's version: Holding a genre or colour in the guide.

Options

OptionWhat it doesDefault
--genre TEXTHolds the genre of the scanned tracks whose path contains TEXT.none
--colour TEXTHolds their colour. --color works too.none
--releaseWith --genre or --colour: takes those tracks off the hold list.off

One at a time: --genre or --colour, not both.

edit

Sets one track's colour, key, genre, hype or BPM by hand, in the app and in your DJ app.

  • Quit your DJ app, unless --no-traktor
  • Changes: the app's database, and your DJ app's collection (backup first)
  • Undo: --clear, or traktor --undo
  • Needs: a scan
uv run dj-track-analyzer edit "part of the name" --colour Red --genre "Hard Trance"
uv run dj-track-analyzer edit "part of the name"
uv run dj-track-analyzer edit
  • Name the track by its path or text in it that matches one track. With no changes it shows the track's values; with no track at all it lists the tracks edited by hand.
  • Values you set are kept after every scan, regenre and new hype score. A hand colour is kept always and counts as yours when the colour scale learns; a hand genre is held.
  • BPM stays in the app: your DJ app's BPM goes with its beat grid, which this app doesn't write. Hype goes into Traktor's Comment only where the Comment already carries one. Rekordbox gets colour and genre; the key stays in the app.

Options
