Hype, sets and crates

The commands that find tracks by how hype they are, plan a set along a shape of colour and hype, and sort your tracks into warm-up, peak and closing crates: hype, set and crates.

Before you start

  • Run every command from the project folder, as uv run dj-track-analyzer <command>. See Running a command.
  • All of them work on scanned tracks with a colour: run scan first (scan --traktor reads your DJ app's collection). hype needs at least 20 scanned tracks. Nothing here analyses audio again.
  • Colour is your own where you set one, else the predicted one (see Colours). Hype is a score from 1 (chill) to 10 (peak), ranked within your own scanned collection.
  • Without --traktor they only print a table; nothing is written. Everything that writes has --dry-run and an undo.
  • Quit your DJ app before any --traktor or --remove: they refuse while it runs.

On the web page, hype lives on the Hype tab and the crates in the Setup tab's Sync everything; planning a set runs from a terminal for now. The guide's Finding hype tracks and Planning a set walk through it.

hype

Lists tracks by how hype they are, most hype first, with filters for picking the next track in a set. Can also write the score into your DJ app's Comment field, so you can sort by it there.

  • Fine with your DJ app open, unless --traktor
  • Changes: nothing; with --traktor your DJ app's collection (backup first)
  • Undo: hype --traktor --remove, or traktor --undo
  • Needs: a scan of at least 20 tracks
uv run dj-track-analyzer hype --bpm 126 --key 8A
uv run dj-track-analyzer hype --gems
uv run dj-track-analyzer hype --traktor --dry-run
  • The score mixes how rave or deep a track sounds (its style) with its energy (loudness, party, aggressive versus relaxed). On a test collection of about 470 tracks it followed the star ratings well, but it can't predict exact stars.
  • Hype you set with edit wins, also in --traktor.
  • --gems: very hype (8+) but rated 4 stars or less, or played at most once. Most underrated first. Worth a listen and maybe a fifth star.
  • --traktor writes Hype NN into Comment, after your own text there (joined with · ). Show the Comment column in Traktor's track list and sort by it. Comment goes onto Pioneer USB exports; Comment 2 doesn't.
  • An old Hype NN in Comment 2, from earlier versions, is taken out on every write. --clean-comment2 does only that.
  • --traktor refuses while Traktor is set to write tags into your files, since the Hype would land in the files too. --force writes anyway.
  • While a tempo view is loaded in Traktor, hype lists the hype as heard at that tempo. --native uses each track at its own tempo.

set

Plans a set along a shape (an arc) of colour, hard ↔ pretty, and hype, chill ↔ peak, with keys that mix from track to track. Can write it as a playlist into your DJ app.

  • Fine with your DJ app open, unless --traktor or --remove
  • Changes: nothing; with --traktor a playlist in your DJ app (backup first)
  • Undo: set --remove NAME
  • Needs: a scan; at least as many tracks as --tracks left after the filters
uv run dj-track-analyzer set
uv run dj-track-analyzer set --arc Magenta/3,Red/9,Violet/6 --tracks 30
uv run dj-track-analyzer set --traktor --name "Friday warm-up"
  • Every slot gets a target colour and hype from the arc. The planner picks the order that stays closest to them, with a bonus for higher star ratings and a cost for keys that clash, the same artist twice in a row and (with --bpm-step) big BPM jumps.
  • The table shows each slot's aim (colour/hype), the track picked, its key (✗ where it doesn't mix with the track before) and the set's length if every track played in full.
  • Arcs: warmup (pretty and chill → harder and hype), peak, journey (pretty → hard peak → back), closing (hard and hype → pretty and calmer), or your own points like Magenta/3,Red/9, spaced evenly. Colours are Red … Magenta or 1–7, hype 1–10.
  • mine learns the arc from your Traktor History: the average colour and hype in each fifth of your sets. It needs at least 3 sessions of 8 or more known tracks.
  • --start, --end and --exclude take text that matches one track's path or name; too many matches and it asks you to be more specific.
  • --tempo 145 plans with the colours and hype heard at 145 BPM, for when you play everything there with key lock. It needs that tempo view. Keys stay the tracks' own.
  • --traktor writes a playlist into a Sets (Track Analyzer) folder, named Set <date> <arc> unless you pass --name. A playlist of the same name there is replaced; your own playlists aren't touched. Works on the DJ app you chose (Traktor or Rekordbox).
  • --remove NAME takes exactly that playlist out again and nothing else, so it works even while a tempo view is loaded. traktor --undo doesn't undo a set.

crates

Sorts your tracks into three crates by colour and hype, and can write them as playlists into your DJ app.

  • Fine with your DJ app open, unless --traktor or --remove
  • Changes: nothing; with --traktor playlists in your DJ app (backup first)
  • Undo: crates --remove
  • Needs: a scan
uv run dj-track-analyzer crates
uv run dj-track-analyzer crates --traktor --dry-run
uv run dj-track-analyzer crates --traktor
  • Warm-up: Green … Magenta with hype up to 5, the most approachable first. Peak: Red … Yellow with hype 8 or more, the most hype and hardest first. Closing: Blue … Magenta with hype 6 or more, the most hype and prettiest first. A track can be in more than one.
  • They're fixed rules, not learned: on a test collection your History didn't show where in a set a track gets played.
  • --traktor writes them into a Crates (Track Analyzer) folder. Run it again after scanning or recolouring: the crates are replaced in place. Your own playlists aren't touched. The table shows --limit tracks per crate; the playlists get them all.
  • With --tempo 145 they use the colours and hype heard at 145 and are named Warm-up @145 and so on, next to the normal ones. crates --remove --tempo 145 takes those out.
  • Sync everything on the page writes the crates too.
