How to use Puppi3's Track Analyzer

Everything happens in a web page on your own computer: scanning, colours, hype, cue points, beat grids and writing it all into Traktor, each with a preview first and an Undo after. Prefer a terminal? Every button has a command, in Command line. Installation is in README.md.

Open the web page

  1. On a Mac: double-click Start Puppi3's Track Analyzer.command in the project folder. The first time, right-click it and choose Open (macOS asks once about apps from the internet). It installs whatever is missing, then opens the page in your browser.
  2. On Linux: run ./"Start Puppi3's Track Analyzer.command" from a terminal in the project folder.
  3. The page lives at http://127.0.0.1:8766/ and runs only on this computer; no audio leaves it. Close the Terminal window (or press Ctrl-C in it) to stop it.

How changes are made

Browsing changes nothing. The page only writes when you press a button that says so, and every change to your files or Traktor goes the same way:

  1. Preview… shows how many tracks change, with examples, and changes nothing.
  2. Write it makes the change. Traktor's collection is backed up first.
  3. Undo puts it back, as long as Traktor hasn't saved its collection since (it does when it quits).
Quit Traktor before writing

Traktor rewrites its collection when it closes, which would undo the change, so the page refuses to write while Traktor is open. Previews work either way, and the dialog offers Check again once you've quit.

Fine with Traktor open

Scanning, learning your colours and cues, checking beat grids, scanning a tempo view, and browsing every tab. They only read Traktor's files.

Long steps (a scan, training) run in the background: a progress bar and the live log show on the Setup tab and in a bar at the top of every tab, so you can keep browsing. Stop is safe; a scan keeps what it has done and carries on from there next time.

First time: the Setup tab

Work down the Setup tab. Each step shows whether it's done, what it needs, and the command it runs.

  1. Analysis models: Download, once (a few hundred MB).
  2. Your music, scanned: Scan Traktor's collection, or type a folder and press Scan folder. About 10–15 minutes per 500 tracks the first time; later scans only do new and changed files. Hype scores are worked out at the end.
  3. Learn from your own Traktor work (optional): Your colour scale → Learn your colours learns your colour-coding from the tracks you've already coloured (needs 30), and Your cue points → Learn your cue points learns where you put cues and loops (needs 30 tracks with 3+ of your own cues). Until you train them, a starter scale and starter cue model are used.
  4. Extra tools (optional): ffmpeg (loops, samples, exact MP3 timing) and Rubber Band (the tempo view). With Homebrew, the page installs them; without it, it shows the command to run.
  5. Into your files and Traktor: the writers, each with Preview… first. See I bought new tracks and Genres.

Everyday tasks

I bought new tracks

  1. Add them to Traktor, let Traktor analyse them, then quit Traktor.
  2. On the Setup tab, Colour new tracks → Preview…. It analyses the new tracks first (10–20 seconds each), then lists what it would set, e.g. Track.mp3: no colour → Orange. Press Write it. Colours you set yourself are never changed.
  3. Optional, genres: Genre tags in your files, then Genres, colours and Smartlists in Traktor (see Genres).
  4. Optional, hype: Hype in Traktor's Comment 2. It updates every track, because new tracks can shift the ranking a little.
  5. Open Traktor and check the new tracks. Changed your mind? Before opening Traktor, Undo the last change to Traktor at the bottom of the Setup tab.

I recoloured a bunch of tracks

  1. Quit Traktor, so the collection is saved.
  2. Setup tab, Your colour scale → Train again (Learn your colours the first time). About a minute.
  3. The Colours tab then shows the new profile and how well the scale fits (see Reading the Colours tab).

Checking my colours for mistakes

  1. On the Colours tab, Tracks that disagree with their colour lists your colour (Yours) next to what the track Sounds like, how many colours apart they are (Gap) and how sure the model is (Confidence). These are judged by a model that never saw that track's colour.
  2. Listen to the tracks at the top in Traktor, and recolour the ones you agree are off.
  3. Or let the app recolour only the clearest cases, marked over threshold (a gap of 2.5 colours or more, and tracks placed there really had that colour or a neighbour 75% of the time or more): quit Traktor, then Recolour them in Traktor… (also on the Setup tab as Recolour clear disagreements).
  4. A track you want left as it is, whatever the model says: hold its colour (below). It's then marked kept and never recoloured.
  5. Then Train again, as in I recoloured a bunch of tracks.

Understanding my collection

  1. Overview for the big picture. Click any bar to see those tracks.
  2. Tracks to find something: search artist, title, file or album, filter by genre, colour, BPM range or key (mixes with it shows every key that mixes with the one you pick), and click a column to sort.
  3. Click a track for its panel. ⇤ Wide gives the waveform more room.
  4. Mixes well with, at the bottom of the panel: tracks in the same genre within 4% of its BPM, in a key that mixes with it. Untick same genre or change the BPM range to widen it.
  5. For a spreadsheet, open data/collection.csv in Numbers, Excel or LibreOffice. Sort by color_position to go from hardest to prettiest.

Keeping a track's genre or colour as it is

  1. On the Tracks tab, tick the tracks (the header box ticks every track shown) and choose Hold genre or Hold colour. Or use the switches in a track's panel.
  2. From then on the genre tags, Traktor writes, recolouring and the tempo view leave that genre or colour alone. Holding writes nothing at the time. Release it the same way; the Held filter lists them.

Cue points like mine

The picture at the top of this guide is a track's Cues and phrases section: your hot cues on top, suggestions below, loop ideas named by what plays in them.

  1. Teach it once: Setup tab, Your cue points → Learn your cue points. It listens to the tracks you cued yourself first (about 13 minutes the first time). Then Listen to the other tracks so they get suggestions too.
  2. Open a track: Cues and phrases shows its waveform (lows red, mids green, highs blue), your cues numbered on top, suggested ones below (Start, Phrase, Breakdown, Drop…) and up to six loop ideas. Zoom with + / − or pinch, down to a single beat.
  3. Listen first: ▶ Play and the pads jump to your cues and the suggestions (Space, 1-8, ←/→ for a bar, shift+←/→ for a phrase). The ▶ buttons in the list let you compare your cue with a proposed correction (press ⏸ to stop).
  4. Tick the cues (and any yellow corrections of your own cues) you want, then Approve ticked. A correction's list lets you pick a different bar the model found instead of its proposal (★). To put one of your other cues on a suggested cue instead, use move my cue here… next to that suggestion. Each loop has a length menu, set to the length you'd likely give it (learned from your own loops). Press C while it plays to set a new cue on the nearest beat, and the ✕ on a pad to delete that cue from Traktor. Approving writes nothing yet; the Tracks filter Cues approved, not written to Traktor yet lists them.
  5. Save sample on a loop idea saves exactly that loop as a WAV in Traktor's sample folder, ready for a Remix Deck.
  6. Quit Traktor and press Write to Traktor… (in the track's panel or at the top of the Tracks tab). It lists what will change first, then writes, with a backup and an Undo button. New cues only fill free hot-cue slots; your cues only move where you ticked a correction, and are only deleted where you ticked that.
  7. Train again on the Setup tab after cueing more tracks.

Beat grids that sit on the kicks

Cue suggestions, loops and samples all trust Traktor's beat grid. To check every grid once (about 3 minutes, later only new and changed ones), press Check the beat grids on the Setup tab.

  1. In the Tracks tab, filter on Grid off the kicks or drifting and open a track. Its Beat grid section says how far the kick lands from the grid lines.
  2. Listen: ▶ Click on the grid now and ▶ Click on the moved grid loop a few bars with a click on each grid line. Adjust the amount if needed, then Approve, or The grid is right to stop it being proposed.
  3. If it says the track's stem file is off, remake the stem first: Traktor uses one grid for both. "BPM drifts" means Traktor's BPM is slightly off; fix that in Traktor, moving the grid won't.
  4. Quit Traktor and press Write grid fixes to Traktor…. It lists what moves, makes a backup, and moves each grid together with that track's cues and loops, so they stay on the beat. Undo puts it back.

Finding hype tracks

  • Most hype first: the Hype tab. Tick least hype first for the chill end.
  • For the next track in a set: type the BPM (within 4%, change it beside the box) and a key in Mixes with key, e.g. 8A. Pick a colour to stay within one.
  • Underrated tracks: tick gems: very hype (8+) but rated 4★ or less, or played at most once. Worth a listen and maybe a fifth star.
  • In Traktor: quit Traktor, press Write to Traktor's Comment 2…, then show the Comment 2 column in Traktor's track list (right-click the column header) and sort by it. Your own text in Comment 2 stays in front. Take it out… on the Setup tab removes only the Hype part again.

Genres for Traktor and Navidrome

  1. Scan first, so new tracks have genres: Setup tab, Scan Traktor's collection.
  2. Genre tags in your files → Preview…, then Write it. With close runner-up genres too ticked, a track between two genres gets both, e.g. "Acid Trance; Hard Trance". Navidrome, Lexicon and other players pick the tags up on their next scan. Put original tags back… undoes it.
  3. Quit Traktor, then Genres, colours and Smartlists in Traktor → Preview… and Write it. It sets the genres, gives uncoloured tracks their predicted colour (untick colours too to skip that) and builds a Genres (Track Analyzer) Smartlist folder.

Edited the genre rules (dj_genres.json)? Working the genres out again from the saved analysis is a terminal step for now: regenre.

Tested with Traktor Pro 4.5: Traktor opens and saves a collection edited by this app, and the genre Smartlists show the right tracks. If something ever looks wrong, quit Traktor and press Undo the last change to Traktor.

Hearing my catalogue at my play tempo

If you play a set at one tempo with key lock, this shows what the models hear there: every track is stretched to that tempo (pitch kept) and analysed again for genre, moods, colour and hype. Your normal results are never touched.

  1. Once: install rubberband (the time-stretcher) under Extra tools on the Setup tab.
  2. Scan your music normally first, then set the BPM of the Tempo view step (145 by default; any tempo works) and press Scan tempo view. About an hour the first time; later only new tracks. Each tempo is its own view.
  3. The Tempo view tab shows what changes: genres at own tempo next to genres at the view tempo, the most common changes, your colours at that tempo, and the tracks that turn hardest, turn prettiest, and gain the most hype.
  4. Browse it: set This page shows to all tracks at 145 BPM, and the Overview, Tracks and Hype tabs show the view. In a track's panel, at 145 BPM plays the track stretched, as the view hears it.
  5. Optional, switch Traktor to the view: quit Traktor, Load into Traktor… (Tempo view or Setup tab), preview, write. Traktor then shows the genres heard at that tempo, your colour moved by how far the model thinks the track shifts, and the view's hype in Comment 2; the genre Smartlist folder becomes Genres @145 (Track Analyzer). The label at the top of the page shows it's loaded.
  6. Switch back with Switch Traktor back to normal… on the Tempo view tab (or Switch Traktor back… on Setup), Traktor closed. Cue points, plays, History and new tracks from meanwhile all stay; a genre, colour or Comment 2 you changed yourself meanwhile is kept.
  7. Lexicon too: open Lexicon, turn on Settings → Integrations → Local API once, and tick Lexicon too on the Setup tab before loading. Its genre, colour, comment and Energy switch to the view. While it's loaded, don't sync Lexicon → Traktor; syncing to your USB is fine (sync again after switching back).

Keep in mind: the genre, mood and instrument models learned from millions of tracks, each at the tempo it was released at. Almost no house was released at 145, so stretched to 145 a rolling house track sounds to them like the trance and psytrance they did hear at that tempo. Read the view as how the models hear the sped-up track, not as facts. Psytrance, the worst case, is held back: in the view, psy only counts as much as the track already had at its own tempo. Rubber Band also isn't Traktor's own stretcher, so it sounds close but not identical. While a view is loaded in Traktor, the other Traktor writers and colour training refuse to run; switch back first.

My recorded mixes

The Mixes tab shows a recorded set's genre and mood, a loudness timeline, a check of the tracklist against Traktor's history (key heard vs Traktor's, the tempo each track was played at, key lock), and clip ideas. Analysing the recording is a terminal step for now (recorded mixes); save the result in logs/ and it appears on the tab, or drop the saved file onto the tab.

After updating the app

  1. Double-click Start Puppi3's Track Analyzer.command again: it installs new dependencies before opening the page.
  2. Check the Setup tab: if a new model is needed, Analysis models offers the download.
  3. Press Your colour scale → Train again. The first run after an update may fill in new data for every track (about 10–15 minutes); later runs take a minute.
  4. If the update notes say the genre list changed or everything should be analysed again, those are terminal steps.

Reading the page

  • Colours: a ? after a colour means it's predicted, not set by you. Confidence is how often tracks placed at that spot really had that colour, on tracks the model hadn't seen; most are 0.2–0.3, because neighbouring colours are close to a coin flip from the sound alone. The ends of the scale are clearly apart.
  • Hype is 1–10 within your own collection: 10 = more hype than 90% of your tracks. Half the euphoric "rave" sound (trance, hard trance, hands-up, hardstyle rather than deep or tech house), half energy (loud, party, aggressive, not relaxed). It can shift a step when you add many tracks.
  • BPM and key: Traktor's where it has them (you may have fixed the grid by hand), ours beside them. In the Tracks list, a dimmed key in brackets, like 5A (8B), is the key the analyzer heard, shown only when it disagrees with Traktor's (hover for both). Our key agreed with Traktor's on about 57% of a test collection; neither is ground truth.
  • Instruments come from a tagger trained on non-club music: compare tracks, don't read the numbers as absolute amounts.

Reading the Colours tab

  • Average error per colour, within one colour, exactly right: how well the scale fits, tested on tracks it didn't learn from. The error is worked out per colour and averaged, so a small colour like Violet counts as much as Red. On the test collection the scale was 1.17 colours off on average, against 1.63 for guessing the middle colour for every track.
  • Profile per colour: the average of each measurement per colour, darker = highest of the row. ρ is how strongly it moves with your scale: + means more of it toward Magenta, − toward Red. Around ±0.3 or more is a real pattern; under ±0.1 is basically nothing. plays and purchased describe how you use the tracks, not how they sound.
  • Where in a set: from Traktor's History, how often each colour is played in the first, middle and last third of a session.
  • Playlists: your playlists from hardest to prettiest by average colour; Stands out marks a colour that appears there at more than twice its usual rate.
  • Artists: whether one artist's tracks get similar colours (closer than random means yes), and the average colour of your most frequent artists.

How it works

Everything runs on your computer; no audio leaves it. The technical details are in README.md.

1. Listening to each track

scan decodes every file once and measures it with Essentia, an open-source audio library:

  • BPM and key, rounded to whole BPM and shown as Camelot. Where Traktor has its own BPM and key, those are used for searching and mixing; ours fill the gaps. Traktor's BPM is never changed, and its beatgrid only moves when you approve a fix (beat grids).
  • Loudness and how the energy rises and falls through the track.
  • Style: two AI models trained on Discogs (one hears 400 styles, the other 519) listen to the main, loudest part of the track and their opinions are blended.
  • Mood, instruments and vocals: smaller models on top say how aggressive, happy, party, relaxed or sad it sounds, which instruments they hear and whether there are vocals.

The results are saved in cache.db, so each track is only analysed once (until its audio changes or it moves). Files over 12 minutes are treated as DJ mixes and get a timeline instead.

2. From styles to DJ genres

Discogs styles are too fine-grained and named for record shops, so a rule list (dj_genres.json) turns them into DJ genre names. Each rule says which styles count and what BPM range fits; a track slightly outside the range loses points gradually instead of being ruled out. The top three become the track's genres. Edit the rules and regenre redoes the genres in seconds from the saved analysis.

3. Learning your colours

colors train looks at the tracks you've already coloured in Traktor and learns which sounds go with which colour, treating Red to Magenta as a scale. Several combinations of measurements are tried and the one that guesses best on tracks it hasn't seen is kept. On a test collection of about 470 tracks it was off by about one colour on average (versus 1.6 for always guessing the middle colour): it reliably tells Red from Magenta, but neighbouring colours are close to a coin flip. That's why it only fills in missing colours and never overwrites yours unless you ask.

4. Hype

Half "rave sound" (how much the style model hears Trance, Hard Trance, Hands Up, Hardstyle or Tech Trance rather than deep styles) and half energy (loudness, party and aggressive moods, minus relaxed). Each part is ranked within your own collection, so Hype 8 means "more hype than about 70–80% of my tracks", not an absolute value.

5. The tempo view

Played at 145 BPM with key lock, a 124 BPM house track sounds different. tempo scan stretches each track to the target tempo with Rubber Band (pitch kept, like Traktor's key lock) and runs the same analysis again, stored separately in data/at-145/. Your colour is shifted by how much the colour model thinks the track changes, not replaced.

6. Writing to Traktor

Traktor keeps its own copy of track info in collection.nml. The app makes small text edits there (genre, colour, Comment 2 and a Smartlist folder) rather than rewriting the file, so everything else stays byte-for-byte the same. A backup is taken first, and --undo refuses if Traktor changed the file since, so it can't throw away your newer work.

Command line

Every button in the page runs a command, shown while it runs, so you can do the same from a terminal (Terminal, or VS Code's). A few things are terminal-only for now: analysing a recorded mix, cutting clips from its video, working out genres again after editing the rules, a full re-analysis, and your own genres from example folders.

  • Run every command from the project folder in Terminal (or VS Code's terminal): 
    cd ~/puppi3s-dj-tools/track-analyzer
    (or wherever you put it)
  • Every command starts with uv run dj-track-analyzer, for example uv run dj-track-analyzer stats. The copy buttons on this page copy the whole command.
  • Try --dry-run first on anything that changes files. It shows what would happen and changes nothing.
  • To keep a copy of the output, add 2>&1 | tee logs/something.txt to the end of a command. Handy when asking for help.
Quit Traktor first

traktor, colors apply, colors review --apply, hype --traktor, tempo load and tempo native. Traktor rewrites its collection when it closes, which would undo the changes, so the app refuses to run these while Traktor is open (their --dry-run works either way).

Fine with Traktor open

scan, analyze, stats, hype (without --traktor), colors train, colors explain and colors review (without --apply), regenre, and tempo scan, report, refresh, export, nml and status only read Traktor's files.

Tasks from a terminal

The same tasks as above, as commands.

I bought new tracks

  1. Add them to Traktor and let Traktor analyse them.
  2. Quit Traktor.
  3. Preview the colours. This analyses the new tracks first, about 10–20 seconds each; examples lists what it would set, e.g. Track.mp3: no colour → Orange. 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer colors apply --dry-run
  4. Set them: 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer colors apply
  5. Optional, genres too: preview, write the file tags, then update Traktor and its genre Smartlists. 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer tag --multi --dry-run
    uv run dj-track-analyzer tag --multi
    uv run dj-track-analyzer traktor
  6. Optional, hype scores. This updates Comment 2 for all tracks, because new tracks can shift the ranking a little. (If a tempo view is loaded, switch back first with tempo native.) 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer hype --traktor
  7. Open Traktor and check the new tracks. Change any colour you disagree with; your own colours are never overwritten.
  8. Changed your mind? Before opening Traktor again: 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer traktor --undo

I recoloured a bunch of tracks

  1. Quit Traktor, so the collection is saved.
  2. Retrain. About a minute after the first time. 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer colors train 2>&1 | tee logs/colors.txt
  3. Optional: see whether the colour profile changed. 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer colors explain

Checking my colours for mistakes

  1. List the tracks whose sound disagrees most with the colour you gave them: 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer colors review
  2. Listen to the tracks at the top of the list in Traktor, and recolour the ones you agree are off.
  3. Or let the app recolour only the clearest cases. A track qualifies when the model puts it at least 2.5 colours away from yours, and tracks it placed at that spot really were that colour or a neighbour at least 75% of the time. Those show meets_apply_threshold: true.
    1. Preview. This lists only the tracks that would be recoloured: 
      uv run dj-track-analyzer colors review --apply --dry-run
    2. Quit Traktor, then: 
      uv run dj-track-analyzer colors review --apply
      It asks about each track: y recolours it, n keeps your colour, k keeps it and never asks about that track again, a recolours all the rest, c cancels without changing anything. Add --yes to skip the questions.
    3. Stricter: --min-gap 3 or --min-confidence 0.85. Undo with traktor --undo before you open Traktor again (afterwards it refuses unless you add --force, which loses what Traktor saved).
  4. Then retrain, as in I recoloured a bunch of tracks.

Finding hype tracks

  • Most hype first (add --least for the chill end): 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer hype
  • For the next track in a set: tracks within 4% of 126 BPM that mix with 8A, most hype first. Add --colour Yellow to stay within one colour. 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer hype --bpm 126 --key 8A
  • Underrated tracks: very hype (8+) but rated 4★ or less, or played at most once. Worth a listen and maybe a fifth star. 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer hype --gems
  • In Traktor: Traktor closed run the command below (--dry-run first to preview), then show the Comment 2 column in Traktor's track list (right-click the column header) and sort by it. 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer hype --traktor

Genres for Traktor and Navidrome

  1. Analyse new or changed tracks: 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer scan --traktor
  2. Check the genres_multi column in collection.csv. That's what will be written.
  3. Preview, then write the file tags. Navidrome picks them up on its next library scan. 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer tag --multi --dry-run
    uv run dj-track-analyzer tag --multi
  4. Traktor closed Preview, then set the genres in Traktor and build the Genres (Track Analyzer) Smartlist folder: 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer traktor --dry-run
    uv run dj-track-analyzer traktor
  5. To undo: untag for the files, traktor --undo for Traktor.

Edited dj_genres.json (or the app's genre list changed in an update)? Work out every track's genres again from the saved analysis, in seconds, then check the CSV and continue from step 3:

uv run dj-track-analyzer regenre

Tested with Traktor Pro 4.5: Traktor opens and saves a collection edited by this app, and the genre Smartlists show the right tracks. If something ever looks wrong, quit Traktor and run traktor --undo.

Hearing my catalogue at my play tempo

If you play a set at one tempo with key lock, this shows what the models hear there: every track is stretched to that tempo (pitch kept) and analysed again for genre, moods, colour and hype. Your normal results are never touched.

Any tempo works. The default is 145. Add --bpm 140 (or any tempo) to scan, report, export, nml, refresh and load; each tempo is its own view in data/at-<bpm>/. Only one can be loaded at a time, and native switches back whichever one is. The steps below use the default.

  1. Once: install Rubber Band, the time-stretcher. 
    brew install rubberband
    On Linux: sudo apt install rubberband-cli (version 3.0 or newer).
  2. Make sure new tracks are scanned normally first, then analyse at your tempo. About an hour the first time; resumable, and later runs only do new or changed tracks. 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer scan --traktor
    uv run dj-track-analyzer tempo scan 2>&1 | tee logs/tempo-scan.txt
  3. See what changes (see reading tempo report): 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer tempo report
  4. Optional, look at it without touching Traktor: a spreadsheet, or a standalone copy of your collection. Both go to data/at-145/ (at-<bpm>/ for other tempos). 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer tempo export
    uv run dj-track-analyzer tempo nml
  5. Optional, switch Traktor to the view. Quit Traktor, preview, then load: 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer tempo load --dry-run
    uv run dj-track-analyzer tempo load
    Traktor then shows, per track: the genres heard at the view tempo; your colour moved by how far the model thinks the track shifts (uncoloured tracks stay uncoloured); the view's hype in Comment 2. The Genres (Track Analyzer) folder is swapped for Genres @145 (Track Analyzer), and the clock is set to the view tempo (tracks follow it only with Sync on and the clock as master).
  6. Switch back (Traktor closed). Cue points, plays, History and new tracks from while the view was loaded all stay. If you changed a genre, colour or Comment 2 yourself in the meantime, your change is kept and listed under kept. The clock always goes back to your tempo. 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer tempo native
  7. Optional, Lexicon too. Open Lexicon, turn on Settings → Integrations → Local API once, then add --lexicon. Lexicon's genre, colour, comment ("Hype NN" after your text) and Energy (the view's hype, 1–10) switch to the view; native puts Lexicon's own energy back, and a Genres @145 (Track Analyzer) smartlist folder appears. If Traktor already has the view, only Lexicon is switched. While it's loaded, don't sync Lexicon → Traktor or import from Traktor; syncing to your USB is fine (sync again after switching back). 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer tempo load --lexicon --dry-run
    uv run dj-track-analyzer tempo load --lexicon
    uv run dj-track-analyzer tempo native --lexicon
  8. While a view is loaded, hype, stats and export show the view (hype at the view tempo). Add --native to see each track at its own tempo.
  9. Changed dj_genres.json or retrained colours? Redo the view's genres, colours and hype from the saved analysis (seconds): 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer tempo refresh
  10. Not sure which is loaded? 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer tempo status

One track, and recorded mixes

uv run dj-track-analyzer analyze "/path/to/track.mp3"

To get the path: in Finder, hold Option, right-click the file and choose Copy "…" as Pathname. On Linux, drag the file into the terminal window. Keep the quotes around it. Files over 12 minutes are treated as DJ mixes: they get a timeline in 2-minute steps and a readable summary after the JSON (--segments 60 for 1-minute steps).

Checking a recorded mix against what you played: add --tracklist to a Traktor recording. It finds that session's history file, places each track in the recording, and shows per track the key Traktor has against the key heard, the tempo you played it at, whether key lock was on, and names the mix by how it sounds as played, with the speed-up and your mixing (stems, layers, effects) counted. Only the models' habit of calling almost any sped-up track psytrance is held back, as in the tempo view, so scan the tracks first. With tempo scan at about the tempo you played, it also lists what your mixing pushed up or down. For a recording Traktor didn't name, add --start 22:00:00 (when it began).

Posting it as a video: add --chapters --offset 0:14 (how far into the finished video the first track starts: check it in the video itself) for YouTube chapters to paste into the description, and --highlights for two suggested 40-second clips, in video times (--highlights 5 for five, ranked). Then clips <analysis>.json --video final.mp4 makes 720p previews of them to watch, ranked by the audio and by how much moves on screen, and --pick 1 4 makes the ones you choose at full quality next to the video.

After updating the app

  1. Install new dependencies, then download any new models: 
    uv sync
    uv run dj-track-analyzer setup
  2. Run colors explain or colors train. The first run fills in new data for every track (about 10–15 minutes); later runs take seconds.
  3. If the genre list changed, redo the genres from the saved analysis (seconds), then tag and sync as in Genres: 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer regenre
  4. If the update notes say so, re-analyse everything (about half an hour per 500 tracks on an M1 Pro): 
    uv run dj-track-analyzer scan --traktor --rescan 2>&1 | tee logs/scan.txt

collection.csv

One row per track. The useful columns:

  • Basics: artist, title, bpm, key, camelot (measured), traktor_bpm, traktor_camelot (Traktor's), lufs
  • Genre: genres_multi (what tag --multi writes), genre + genre_score, genre_2, genre_3, tag_genre (what the file's tag says now)
  • Colour: traktor_color (yours), color (predicted), color_position, color_confidence. Sort by color_position for hard → pretty.
  • Sound: mood_*, instruments, approachability, hype (1–10, filled by the hype command)
  • Problems: error, if the file couldn't be analysed

colors explain

  • Profile per colour: the average of each measurement per colour.
    • corr is how strongly it moves with your scale: + means more of it toward Magenta, − more toward Red. Around ±0.3 or more is a real pattern; under ±0.1 is basically nothing.
    • plays is the median play count and purchased the share whose comment says it was purchased (e.g. "Purchased at Beatport.com"). Those describe how you use the tracks, not how they sound.
  • Strongest links: the measurements that match your colours best, strongest first.
  • Playlists: your playlists sorted from hardest to prettiest by average colour. * marks a colour that appears in that list at more than twice its usual rate.
  • Where in a set: from Traktor's History. For each colour, how often it's played in the first, middle and last third of a session.
  • Artists: whether one artist's tracks get similar colours (a lower "differ by" than random means yes), and the average colour of your most frequent artists.

colors train

  • baseline_always_guess_middle: how good guessing your middle colour for every track would be (1.63 colours off on the test collection), with the same four numbers as the model. The model should beat it clearly.
  • average_error_in_colours: how many colour steps off it is on average, tested on tracks it didn't learn from. On the test collection: 1.17, against 1.63 for the baseline.
  • exact: how often it guesses exactly your colour.
  • within_one_colour: how often it's right or one step off (67% on the test collection).
  • average_error_per_colour: the same error, but worked out for each colour separately and then averaged, so a small colour like Violet counts as much as a big one like Red. Training keeps the model with the lowest value here: the plain average would favour a model that plays safe in the middle and never gets the end colours right. 0 would be perfect; your own colouring isn't perfectly consistent either, so don't expect it near 0.
  • accuracy_per_colour: The two ends are hardest to get exactly (around 10–13% on the test collection), because the model plays it safe toward the middle. Neighbouring colours are close to a coin flip from the sound alone.
  • biggest_disagreements: tracks worth a second listen (also in colors review).

colors review

  • your_colour vs sounds_like, and gap: how many colours apart they are.
  • confidence: how often tracks placed at that spot were that colour or a neighbour.
  • meets_apply_threshold: whether --apply would recolour it. apply_threshold.tracks_meeting_it counts them.

hype

  • hype: 10 = more hype than 90% of your tracks, 1 = the chillest 10%. It's ranked within your own collection, so it can shift a step when you add many tracks.
  • stars and plays come from Traktor. A ? after the colour means it's predicted, not set by you.
  • What counts as hype: half the euphoric "rave" sound (trance, hard trance, hands-up, hardstyle, rather than deep or tech house), half energy (loud, party, aggressive, not relaxed). Drop size doesn't count: in testing it didn't match star ratings at all. Melodic, progressive trance scores low: pretty but not pushing.
  • With --traktor: comment2_updated is how many tracks changed, and kept_your_text how many already had text in Comment 2 (kept in front of the Hype part).

tempo report

  • First line: how many tracks, and how much they were sped up or slowed down to reach the view tempo (at 145: +15% median).
  • Top genre: genre counts normally and at the view tempo, how many tracks change top genre, and the most common changes (each line: number of tracks, then normal genre → genre at the view tempo).
  • Colour: for your coloured tracks, the average shift (− = harder, + = prettier), how many land on another colour, and per colour of yours where they end up.
  • Turn hardest / Stay prettiest / turn prettier: the biggest colour shifts each way. Most hype at 145 and Biggest hype risers: hype at the view tempo, and the tracks that gain most. Each line shows BPM, your colour → colour in the view (shift), hype normal → in the view and the genres.

colors apply / traktor

  • colours_set: how many tracks got a colour. colours_kept: tracks that already had your colour (untouched).
  • no_colour_prediction: uncoloured tracks it couldn't predict, usually files that failed to analyse.
  • examples: the first changes, e.g. Track.mp3: no colour → Orange.
  • changed: false means nothing needed doing. backup is where the copy of the old collection went.
